# Nordcenter ApS — CCTV Data Protection Impact Assessment

**Privacy & GRC Portfolio Project | Fictional Case Study | Work in Progress**

## Project overview

This project documents an ongoing GDPR Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) exercise concerning a proposed CCTV system at **Nordcenter ApS**, a fictional shopping centre operator in Aarhus, Denmark.

The proposed system involves **120 fixed cameras** operating in selected publicly accessible common areas of a shopping centre receiving approximately **80,000 visitors per week**. Its purposes are to help deter and detect security incidents, including theft, vandalism and violence, and to support the investigation and documentation of specific incidents.

The project demonstrates a structured approach to defining a processing activity, establishing the scope of a DPIA and documenting privacy considerations under **Article 35 of the GDPR**. The planned large-scale systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas engages **Article 35(3)(c)**.

## Project status — Work in Progress

**The DPIA is not yet complete.** The published main DPIA currently covers the initial assessment sections up to and including the scope reference. The detailed scope is presented in a separate supporting document.

The subsequent assessment of necessity and proportionality, risks to individuals, safeguards and residual risks will be added as the project progresses. The preliminary scope remains pending validation, and outstanding questions have been recorded rather than answered through simulated stakeholder consultations.

## Scope at a glance

The assessment concerns CCTV-related personal-data processing in Nordcenter's designated common areas, including recording, local storage, access and review, restricted maintenance access by **SecureVision ApS**, potential disclosure to Danish police, and retention and deletion.

The case does **not** propose facial recognition, AI-based behaviour analysis, marketing tracking or employee performance monitoring. Incidental recording of employees and other individuals present in monitored areas nevertheless remains relevant to the assessment.

## Method and limitations

This is an **independent portfolio exercise based on a fictional case**, not an assessment commissioned or approved by a real organisation.

- The analysis is based on the case study and supporting working notes.
- AI was used to generate the fictional case and support research, draft review and editorial revision; the process is described in **Methodology and Approach**.
- No real or simulated consultations with internal departments, the processor or other stakeholders have been conducted.
- Information that cannot be established from the case is identified as **outstanding or requiring validation**, rather than presented as a confirmed fact.
- No formal validation or approval has taken place. The scope document is **preliminary — pending validation**. It defines what is considered in the DPIA but does not itself establish the lawfulness, necessity or residual risks of the processing.

## Regulatory framework

The project draws on:

- **GDPR**, particularly Article 35 (Data Protection Impact Assessments).
- **European Data Protection Board (EDPB)** guidance on processing personal data through video devices.
- Relevant **Danish data protection and CCTV surveillance legislation and guidance**.

## Disclaimer

Nordcenter ApS, SecureVision ApS and the scenario described in the case study are fictional for the purposes of this portfolio exercise. This project is educational and does not constitute legal advice or a validated operational DPIA.
