# Nordcenter ApS — CCTV Data Protection Impact Assessment

**Privacy & GRC Portfolio Project | Fictional Case Study | Work in Progress**

## Project overview

This project documents an ongoing GDPR Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) exercise concerning a proposed CCTV system at **Nordcenter ApS**, a fictional shopping centre operator in Aarhus, Denmark.

The proposed system involves **120 fixed cameras** operating in selected publicly accessible common areas of a shopping centre receiving approximately **80,000 visitors per week**. Its purposes are to help deter and detect security incidents, including theft, vandalism and violence, and to support the investigation and documentation of specific incidents.

The project demonstrates a structured approach to understanding a processing activity, determining whether a DPIA is required, defining its scope and documenting outstanding questions under **Article 35 of the GDPR**. The planned large-scale systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas engages **Article 35(3)(c)**.

## Project status — Work in Progress

**The DPIA is not yet complete.** The currently published main DPIA document covers the initial sections up to and including the scope reference. The detailed scope is provided in a separate supporting document.

The later assessment stages — including necessity and proportionality, risks to individuals' rights and freedoms, safeguards, and residual risk — have not yet been published. They are intended to be developed as the project continues.

The scope is **preliminary and pending validation**. Outstanding questions have been documented, not resolved through invented stakeholder responses.

## Scope at a glance

The assessment concerns CCTV-related personal-data processing in Nordcenter's designated common areas, including recording, local storage, access and review, restricted maintenance access by **SecureVision ApS**, potential disclosure to Danish police, and retention and deletion.

The case does **not** propose facial recognition, AI-based behaviour analysis, marketing tracking or employee performance monitoring. Incidental recording of employees and other people present in monitored areas nevertheless remains relevant to the assessment.

## Method and limitations

This is an **independent portfolio exercise based on a fictional case**, not an assessment commissioned or approved by a real organisation.

- The exercise began with research into DPIA requirements and a practical scoping method.
- AI was used to generate the fictional case and to assist with reviewing, revising and improving the author's research notes and documentation. The detailed process is described in **Methodology and Approach**.
- The analysis is based on the fictional case study and supporting working notes.
- No real or simulated consultations with internal departments, the processor or other stakeholders have been conducted.
- Information not established by the case is identified as **outstanding or requiring validation**, rather than presented as confirmed fact.
- No formal scope approval or final DPIA validation has taken place.

## Regulatory framework

The project draws on:

- **GDPR**, particularly Article 35 (Data Protection Impact Assessments).
- **European Data Protection Board (EDPB)** guidance on processing personal data through video devices.
- Relevant **Danish data protection and CCTV surveillance legislation and guidance**.

## Disclaimer

Nordcenter ApS, SecureVision ApS and the circumstances described in the case study are fictional for the purposes of this portfolio exercise. This project is educational and does not constitute legal advice or a validated operational DPIA.
