# Article 15 DSAR — EU Representative Perspective

**Independent Privacy Portfolio Project | Fictional Case | Assessment Exercise**

## Project overview

This project examines the operational handling of a **data subject access request (DSAR) under GDPR Article 15** from the perspective of an **EU representative under Article 27**.

In the fictional case, **Emma Nielsen**, a customer in Denmark, sends an access request to **EU Privacy Representation Services GmbH**, the designated EU representative of **Northstar Outfitters LLC**, a US-based e-commerce controller. The request covers order history, customer service correspondence, marketing personal data, other personal data, recipient information, and retention periods.

The exercise demonstrates how I evaluate a request, distinguish legal requirements from practical choices, and propose a traceable handling process. **Northstar remains the controller responsible for the substantive response**; the EU representative's coordination activities depend on its mandate.

## Areas assessed

1. Receive the request
2. Register the request
3. Classify the request
4. Initial assessment and identity considerations
5. Forward to the controller
6. Manage deadlines
7. Acknowledge receipt
8. Monitor progress
9. Escalate issues
10. Coordinate the response
11. Confirm completion
12. Close and document

These stages form a **practical model**, not a legally mandated sequence. In real workflows, several stages may overlap.

## Approach and limitations

- I examined the fictional case step by step, identified relevant issues, and documented my assessments, decisions, and proposed operational actions.
- The analysis refers to the **GDPR**, notably Articles 12, 15, and 27, and relevant **EDPB guidance** on access rights and EU representation.
- A working deadline of **16 December 2026** is used based on receipt by the designated representative on **16 November 2026**; the decision record explains this operational assumption.
- The later-stage scenarios involving delays, escalation, and draft-response review are **hypothetical decision exercises**, not events that actually took place.
- No real data subject, client, supervisory authority, or stakeholder was contacted. No identity check, forwarding, acknowledgement, response, or closure was actually performed.
- The representative's practical responsibilities depend on the agreed mandate; this is **not a description of Prighter's internal procedure**.
- AI tools assisted with fictional scenario development, drafting, research support, and editorial/juridical review. The assessments reflect the case-based decisions developed for this portfolio project; no external lawyer or organisation validated the exercise.
